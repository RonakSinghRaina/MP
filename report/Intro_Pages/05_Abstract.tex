\noindent
Radio frequency interference (RFI) from mobile phones, satellites, aircraft and
other human technology is often much stronger than the faint signals that radio
telescopes observe. It corrupts the data and has to be identified and removed,
a step called flagging, before any scientific analysis. Since modern telescopes
produce far too much data to inspect by hand, this flagging has to be automatic.

In this work, we treat RFI flagging as an image segmentation problem on
spectrograms. We reproduce the U-Net method of Akeret et al.\ (\tfunet{}), which
reaches an \Fone{} of 0.93 on our synthetic data, and design an improved
network, \hybrid{}. On real LOFAR observations labelled by a human expert,
\hybrid{} reaches a maximum \Fone{} of $0.6603 \pm 0.0040$ with 593{,}842
parameters, higher than the best method in Mesarcik et al.'s comparison
(RFI-Net, 0.5979). Fine-tuning it on
expert labels raises this further to 0.675 under cross-validation. However,
removing the components it was designed around, namely the strip convolutions,
channel attention and residual blocks, leaves its score unchanged. Finally,
\tfunet{} scores 0.44 lower on real data than on synthetic data, which shows
that real observations are far more complex than synthetic benchmarks.
